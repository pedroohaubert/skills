# Message

The user pastes this into a chat or an email and sends it as their own. The reader did not follow the work, so the message stands alone.

1. **Reader.** Name who receives it; infer from the conversation when the request does not say. The reader sets the vocabulary: business readers get the names of screens, fields, and reports; developers get git terms and file names when those help.
2. **Facts.** Collect what happened, why, and what happens next.
3. **Case.** Read the one file that matches and write the message from it:

   | The message | Read |
   |---|---|
   | Tells what happened or changed, and why; or reports a problem and asks the reader about it | `cases/explain.md` |
   | Lists what was delivered, released, or moved to testing | `cases/status.md` |
   | Asks a business expert to decide or confirm the rules of the work | `cases/questions.md` |

4. **Register.** Short and direct, the way the user talks to this person, in the language they use with them: first person for what the user did, everyday words as said aloud to a colleague, and plain text where commas, periods, and parentheses carry the structure.
5. **Length.** Short is the default: the shortest version the reader answers on one read. When the reader already has the context (they asked, they are waiting on this item, they are in the thread), the message is one or two sentences: the answer or the ask, and the options. Otherwise use the case's shape, and leave out what the reader already knows and how the work was done. "Shorter", or a message for one person's private chat, means cutting examples and side facts while keeping every step of the cause. Context comes back only when the reader asks for it; a reader who says it is confusing gets the missing step back, in its own sentence.

Done when someone who was not in the conversation understands the message on one read.

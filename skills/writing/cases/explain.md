# Explain

Tells the reader what happened, in the order it happened, and closes on what happens now.

Short paragraphs of two to four sentences, in this order:

1. **What happened**, in the first sentence.
2. **Why.** The whole chain of cause, one step per sentence. This is the part that keeps the reader from assuming carelessness. When the cause is still unknown, give what you checked and what it showed.
3. **What holds now**, or what will be done, by whom, and when.
4. **The question or request**, when there is one, last.

A group message opens by addressing the group and explains enough for someone arriving now. An item from a list or board carries its number and its subject in parentheses the first time: "item 34 (shipping estimate per region)".

## Sizes

| Asked for | Shape |
|---|---|
| Default | Three or four short paragraphs |
| One person's private chat | The same order in one or two paragraphs |
| A summary of the problem | One paragraph of two or three sentences: what was wrong, in the reader's terms, and how it was solved |

## Example

Request: explain to the business group why item 34 was not in the release.

Team, here is why item 34 (shipping estimate per region) did not go out.

Items 16, 21, and 34 touch the same part of the checkout, so they were built together, in one package. On the release of the 7th, item 21 was approved but 34 was still in testing. So I took 34 out of the package and shipped the rest. Item 16 went along with 21 even without approval, because the two share a rule and could not be separated.

The only release since then was on the 9th, and it carried just the discount fix. Item 34 stayed in testing, waiting for the next one.

I will ship 34 tomorrow. From now on each item gets its own package, so one never ships by dragging another or gets stuck because of it.
